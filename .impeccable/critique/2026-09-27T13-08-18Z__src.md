## Design Specificity Verdict

The Calm Card Table identity is visible in the cool atmosphere, editorial headings, paper surfaces,
language pills, and flip/swipe review interaction. The app nevertheless feels split between restrained
blue editorial management, orange-led search, and emerald/rose plus Contrail-led home/review. The main
opportunity is to make the daily study ritual and language context the connective identity across routes.

The detector returned 41 findings: 2 side-tab warnings, 11 color advisories, 17 font-size advisories,
and 11 radius advisories. Several are documentation mismatches because DESIGN.md explicitly sanctions
the leading borders, management radii, review-card radius, and multiple flagged type sizes. Remaining
findings indicate real cross-route token drift. Mutable browser injection failed, so no reliable visual
overlay was presented; CLI output and fresh browser/DOM evidence were used instead.

## Overall Impression

The product has a compelling signature interaction and unusually trustworthy failure handling. Its
largest weakness is the absence of one continuous visual and interaction grammar.

## What's Working

- Failure-state design is safe, specific, and unusually trustworthy.
- The shared English/Japanese management model remains understandable.
- Flip-before-answer, card stacking, swipe feedback, and submission form a distinctive review ritual.

## Priority Issues

### [P1] Core review interaction is undiscoverable and not keyboard-operable

The card is an imperative clickable/dragged div, answer buttons begin disabled without explanation,
and the answer labels are ambiguous. Make the card semantic and keyboard-operable, add concise reveal
guidance, provide 1-4 or arrow shortcuts, and rename choices to Forgot / Hard / Almost / Knew.

Suggested command: `$impeccable harden src/lib/components/SwipeCards.svelte`

### [P1] Card authoring is a flat wall of optional fields

Roughly 14 inputs, multiple textareas, 15 part-of-speech values, and comma-separated lexical fields
create unnecessary cognitive load. Lead with required learning content, progressively disclose
Example / Word details / Notes, and replace comma syntax with token inputs.

Suggested command: `$impeccable distill src/lib/components/CardForm.svelte`

### [P2] Visual and interaction grammar fractures by route

Blue management, orange search, emerald home/review, competing type roles, and inconsistent control
treatments weaken the documented identity. Keep Study Blue primary, reserve other colors for semantic
state, use one editorial moment per page, and consolidate shared primitives.

Suggested command: `$impeccable polish src`

### [P2] Home and empty states under-serve the daily ritual

Home does not frame what is due or which language is active, while several empty states offer no useful
next step. Add a compact Today card and action-oriented routes forward.

Suggested command: `$impeccable onboard src`

### [P2] Learner-facing screens expose implementation terminology

Version, Stage, match percentages, index coverage, and “a bit” answers force translation of internal
mechanics. Replace them with learner-facing scheduling, confidence, and coverage language.

Suggested command: `$impeccable clarify src`

## Persona Red Flags

**Jordan, first-time user:** Disabled review icons and no reveal instruction create uncertainty on the
first card; empty states do not consistently teach the next action.

**Sam, accessibility-dependent user:** The review card is not tabbable or announced as interactive;
swiping lacks a keyboard equivalent and answer feedback leans heavily on color and icon shape.

**Alex, power user:** Review has no keyboard shortcuts or undo, and card authoring requires traversing
every optional field.

## Cognitive Load

Four of eight checks fail: chunking, visual hierarchy, minimal choices, and progressive disclosure.
The 15-item part-of-speech list and approximately 14 authoring fields are the largest choice sets.

## Emotional Journey

Home begins calm but vacant. Review supplies the best emotional peak but starts with an unexplained
interaction valley. Management feels safe until the long authoring form becomes bureaucratic.

## Questions to Consider

- Is the emotional peak knowing one word or completing today's ritual?
- Would removing orange from ordinary controls strengthen Study Blue?
- Why require optional metadata when term and meaning already make a useful card?
- Should four answers describe confidence explicitly, or should scheduling infer nuance from binary input?
