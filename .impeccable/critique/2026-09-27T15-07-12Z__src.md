## Design Specificity Verdict

The redesign now feels authored for this product at the system level. The dated personal welcome,
cool blue atmosphere, editorial headings, tactile review metaphor, language-aware routes, and
progressive card authoring belong together as “The Calm Card Table.”

The remaining break is concentrated rather than systemic: Review—the product’s signature
ritual—still looks and behaves like an older micro-app inside the redesigned shell. Its slate
surfaces, dominant rose/emerald controls, Contrail typography, generic confetti, and modal styling
weaken the continuity established by Home, Search, and management.

The deterministic scan found 74 findings: 3 warnings and 71 advisories. These comprised 37
font-size advisories, 23 color advisories, 11 radius advisories, 2 side-tab warnings, and 1
layout-transition warning. The two side-tab warnings are false positives because the documented
system explicitly permits the blue management rule and amber recovery rule. Many size and radius
advisories are documentation-parser mismatches. Valid findings concentrate in review palette drift
and Search’s padding animation, which causes horizontal movement on hover.

No reliable user-visible overlay is available. Browser mutation failed because the automation
surface exposed document.title as getter-only, so the overlay script was not injected. Desktop Home
inspection succeeded; authenticated routes redirected to sign-in, and the mobile viewport override
did not produce a reliable capture.

## Overall Impression

This is now a coherent, personal learning product rather than a set of adjacent CRUD screens. The
largest opportunity is to finish the signature review ritual: make swipe semantics truthful, add a
correction path, reserve celebration for confirmed completion, and bring its visuals fully into the
blue paper system.

## What’s Working

- The homepage establishes a calm daily ritual with one obvious action and useful secondary routes.
- Card authoring leads with required learning content and progressively discloses optional detail.
- Recovery behavior distinguishes unknown, rejected, conflicted, and saved outcomes while preserving work.
- Search preserves language context, avoids similarity jargon, and offers a fallback for incomplete semantic coverage.

## Priority Issues

### [P1] Two-direction swiping conflicts with four-result grading

**Why it matters:** A left swipe always records `no` and a right swipe always records `yes`, while
the visible scale offers Forgot, Hard, Almost, and Knew it. “Swipe or press 1–4” implies equivalent
precision, and there is no undo. This can silently create the wrong scheduling record.

**Fix:** Explicitly teach “Swipe left = Forgot; right = Knew it. Use buttons or 1–4 for nuance,” add
a one-step Undo, and show an answer summary before saving. Persist unfinished answers if review
interruption is in scope.

**Suggested command:** `$impeccable shape src/routes/review`

### [P1] Small muted text is below reliable contrast on blue surfaces

**Why it matters:** `#60788c` is used around 0.68–0.82rem and measures roughly 3.17:1 against the
darkest atmosphere and 4.12:1 against solid blue paper—below 4.5:1 for normal text. Scope,
pronunciation, status, and helper copy are the content most likely to disappear for low-vision users.

**Fix:** Promote small metadata to `#405c73` or introduce a darker muted token around `#455f73`,
then verify every translucent-paper/background combination.

**Suggested command:** `$impeccable audit src`

### [P2] Celebration appears before completion is confirmed

**Why it matters:** The last answered card shows confetti and says answers are ready to submit, then
confirmed submission produces another confetti state. The emotional peak happens twice, and the
first one can be mistaken for saved progress.

**Fix:** Replace the pre-submit celebration with a compact “10 cards answered” review-and-save
state. Reserve confetti and completion language for confirmed server success.

**Suggested command:** `$impeccable delight src/routes/review/+page.svelte`

### [P2] Review remains visually outside the documented system

**Why it matters:** The most memorable surface has the weakest connection to Study Blue, paper
surfaces, and the editorial hierarchy. The redesign therefore still feels partially applied.

**Fix:** Restyle the review card, progress, answer controls, completion state, and supplementary-info
modal within the Calm Card Table system. Keep rose and emerald as momentary semantic feedback, not
dominant surfaces.

**Suggested command:** `$impeccable polish src/routes/review`

### [P2] Deck management still exposes too many peer controls and backend vocabulary

**Why it matters:** Back navigation, language switching, three CRUD actions, status tabs, and card
navigation compete in one viewport. `zh-TW explanations · Version 3` also exposes implementation
detail during ordinary use.

**Fix:** Keep New card dominant; quiet or group Edit/Archive as deck settings. Translate locale
codes into human labels and hide version metadata outside conflict recovery.

**Suggested command:** `$impeccable distill src/routes/decks/[deckId]/+page.svelte`

## Persona Red Flags

**Alex — power user:** Review shortcuts are excellent, but a mistaken numeric answer cannot be
undone. The flow pauses after the final answer for a second submission action despite already
celebrating. Management has no batch archive or multi-select path.

**Sam — accessibility-dependent user:** Skip navigation, visible focus, live announcements, and
labeled review controls are strong. Small muted text fails contrast. The review card uses
`role="button"` while containing an info button, creating fragile nested-interactive semantics.
Swipe guidance does not explain how two directions map to four ratings.

**Casey — distracted mobile user:** Answer controls are well positioned and sized, but unsubmitted
progress can be lost on refresh. The review route’s `h-dvh` container inside the global shell may
push controls beyond the first viewport. Header targets remain slightly below 44px.

## Cognitive Load

Overall cognitive load is low: 1 of 8 checks fails. The remaining failure is minimal choices on
deck-detail screens, where back navigation, languages, CRUD actions, status filters, and card
navigation compete. Review and Search remain within the four-choice working-memory threshold.

## Emotional Journey

The opening is personal, calm, and achievable. Review creates momentum through a tactile, focused
interaction. The emotional valley occurs after the last answer, when celebration precedes the
still-required server submission. Failure recovery is the strongest emotional design because it is
honest and preserves work. The empty “nothing due” state remains friendly but lacks a useful next
action.

## Minor Observations

- Search uses `JP` while language tabs use `JA`.
- `1 / 10` should read “Card 1 of 10.”
- “Submit Results” should use sentence case and name the consequence, such as “Save answers.”
- The no-due-cards state needs a route home or to decks.
- Search hover changes horizontal padding, causing content shift; use transform or background only.
- Review dates use `zh-TW` regardless of the surrounding English UI.
- Externally loaded display fonts may visibly swap under poor network conditions.

## Questions to Consider

- Should swipe remain the fast binary path while buttons and 1–4 own nuanced grading?
- Is the truthful completion moment the last answer, or the server-confirmed save?
- If Review is the signature ritual, should it receive the strongest expression of the Calm Card Table?
- How much unfinished review progress is acceptable to lose during a mobile interruption?
